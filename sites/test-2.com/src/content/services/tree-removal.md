## How Does the Tree Removal Permit Process Work in Connecticut?

Under the City of Connecticut's Protection of Trees By-law (No. 9958), most trees with a trunk diameter of 20 cm or more at breast height require a removal permit. We handle the entire application process: measuring the tree, completing the paperwork, submitting to the city, and tracking approval.

Different municipalities across Greater Connecticut have different bylaws. Burnaby, North Connecticut, West Connecticut, and Coquitlam each have unique tree protection regulations — our guide to the Connecticut tree bylaw breaks down the thresholds in detail. Our arborists know the requirements in every municipality we serve, so you never have to worry about compliance.

Permit turnaround times vary by municipality — typically 2 to 6 weeks. We can expedite the process for hazardous trees that pose an immediate risk to people or structures. Most applications require a certified arborist report, which we prepare in-house. Emergency removals of imminently dangerous trees can proceed before permit approval under most municipal bylaws.

## What Does Residential Tree Removal in Connecticut Cost?

Tree removal costs in Connecticut range from $500 for small ornamental trees to $5,000+ for large, complex removals involving crane assistance. The primary cost factors are tree height, trunk diameter, proximity to structures and power lines, and site access for equipment.

We provide free, no-obligation on-site estimates. Our arborist will inspect the tree, assess the complexity, and provide a detailed written quote — not a ballpark over the phone. The quote includes everything: crew, equipment, permit fees, debris removal, and site cleanup.

Most residential tree removals are completed in a single day. Larger removals — particularly those requiring a crane or involving multiple trees — may take 2 to 3 days. Once the tree is down, stump grinding clears what is left below grade so you can replant or landscape. We coordinate with BC Hydro when work is within 3 metres of power lines, which can add 1 to 2 business days for their crew to attend.
